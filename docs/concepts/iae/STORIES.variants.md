# Stories: variants

Writing as a graph: every change forks, consequences are worked out,
every line colored. Nothing is a single revision.

- As an author, I never lose a revision, scene, draft, or vignette; every
  version, variation, and rewrite is kept.
- As an author, I can select any version by a click and swap it in.
- As an author, I can compose a passage out of pieces of several versions.
- As an author, I can blend versions together.
- As an author, I can go back to an earlier revision and keep its tone.
- As an author, I can keep a single phrase from an earlier revision.
- As an author, I can strike through a line without losing it.
- As an author, I can leave a note on a passage to make it more loving,
  salty, truthful, deceitful, et cetera.
- As an author, I can pose a story-level choice -- "Should Joe die here,
  or in Act 3?" -- and have the IAE help me consider each possibility.
- As an author, I can pose a line-level choice -- "Should she say yes?
  Fuck no? Hell no? Maybe?" -- and see the options side by side.
- As an author, I get help synthesizing, analyzing, and evaluating the
  options, not just generating them.
- As an author, I see my writing as a graph; every change I make forks
  off new potential revisions rather than overwriting the old one.
- As an author, I can make a story-level change -- "kill Joe in Act 2,
  not Act 3" -- and the IAE figures out its impact on plot structure,
  character knowledge, and character emotion without my tracing it.
- As an author, I am told when a later scene no longer works on this
  branch -- Joe's apartment in Act 3 when Joe died in Act 2 -- or needs at
  least a change in tone.
- As an author, I can see each line of the graph colored by its state:
  green, red, orange, and others; a non-green line tells me it is
  violating a constraint, and which kind.
